## Blocking findings

### [high] T21R2-01 — A disarm acknowledgement does not keep a downgraded copy off

**Location:** `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunner.ts:208–213,231–237`; `apps/server/src/project/CoordinatorPlannedHandover.ts:273–286,644–656`; `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskCopies.ts:85–96`.

**Scenario:** A and B settle with A the runner and B physically off. Downgrade B to v1.0.379. A then hands its coordinator to B normally, without moving the routine. A's outgoing snapshot includes the enabled routine and due slot. The [released receiver applies both before takeover](https://github.com/anton-abyzov/specwithcode/blob/v1.0.379/apps/server/src/project/CoordinatorPlannedHandover.ts#L412-L425); its [replica writer](https://github.com/anton-abyzov/specwithcode/blob/v1.0.379/apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskCopies.ts#L253-L287) writes `enabled: true`. B is now the sole active coordinator and can fire. A still has B's acknowledgement and, with the T21-02 fix, also fires despite its transferred binding. The same slot is eligible twice. This is **not** the accepted partition between two stale legacy owners: the handover succeeded. With two released machines A would stop.

There is a second way to make that acknowledgement unsound: `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunnerReceive.ts:173,245–279` turns a failed row lookup into “no row.” A transient lookup or decode failure on an existing, enabled B row skips the physical disarm at `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunner.ts:793–823` but can still return `acked`. The new runner record alone is not a fence v1.0.379 reads.

**Fix and regression test:** Make outgoing legacy snapshots incapable of arming a settled runner's resting copy, or restore the legacy exclusion before allowing such a snapshot. Fail a disarm closed on an unreadable row; distinguish it from confirmed absence. Run the pinned old receiver's *replica/handover write* between acknowledgement and due selection; require one fire, on A. Fail the lookup separately and require no acknowledgement.

### [high] T21R2-02 — Rearming overwrites a due slot the coordinator already carried

**Location:** `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunner.ts:502–511,786–823`; `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskService.ts:1812–1829`; `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/Schedule.ts:31–36`.

**Scenario:** An hourly routine is due at 12:05. Before settlement, B turns its copy off and its acknowledgement is lost. A hands the coordinator to B; B can receive the actual 12:05 slot in the handover (`apps/server/src/project/CoordinatorPlannedHandover.ts:531–562`). At 12:04 A reaches its persisted recipient and rearms B. `followAgain` calls the ordinary user-facing enable operation. That sets the next slot to **13:04**, not 12:05. A no longer coordinates and never settled; B is the only eligible runner. Both are online at 12:05 and neither fires. In v1.0.379 the handover installs the carried slot and there is no second enable to overwrite it. The new lost-answer tests hide the difference by writing `next_run_at` after recovery.

**Fix and regression test:** Restore the effective enabled state without treating recovery as a new user arming. Preserve the authoritative due slot and occurrence state across disarm and rearm. Put 12:05 on the rows **before** disarm, hand over, rearm at 12:04, advance the clock to 12:05, and count exactly one dispatch without rewriting either row. The same restore must not increment a one-shot's generation as a user rearm would.

### [high] T21R2-03 — A copy can still authorize a stale edit with the current revision

**Location:** `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunnerReceive.ts:215–254`; `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunner.ts:480–494,538–540,1179–1190`.

**Scenario:** A is at revision 7 with a new prompt. B has no row yet. Disarm discards the accompanying definition because there is no row, but records revision 7 and acknowledges. A delayed, revisionless coordinator replica subsequently supplies the old prompt. B saves it off and retains revision 7. A person on B edits only the title before a correcting refresh. The form forwards the old prompt with `expectedRevision: 7`; on A the [equality check](https://github.com/anton-abyzov/specwithcode/blob/48a14393843a21fb74fce1013c0dac945edcef14/apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunnerReceive.ts#L375-L382) succeeds and the [save](https://github.com/anton-abyzov/specwithcode/blob/48a14393843a21fb74fce1013c0dac945edcef14/apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunnerReceive.ts#L418-L451) overwrites the newer prompt. Both peers can be online and authorized. The same false pairing is possible with both rows present: reconciliation reads the tasks and revision map separately, so a save between the reads pairs the old definition with the new count at `apps/server/src/scheduledTasks/ScheduledTaskRunner.ts:441–453`. Its successful delivery can even mark that revision as already pushed (lines 600–610).

**Fix and regression test:** Read each definition and revision from one consistent snapshot. Track “acknowledged disarm” separately from “installed definition at revision N”; never accept edits based on an unverified pair. Test both a late first replica and an edit between the sender's reads. A title-only edit must either preserve the new prompt or be refused. A matching counter alone is not proof.
